CAPTURE (STIMULATION) THRESHOLD
  1. VVI (atrium: AAI); rate +10 bpm over intrinsic so box paces 1:1.
  2. From a capturing output, decrease slowly until capture lost — spike, no P/QRS, loss of art/SpO₂.
  3. Increase until capture returns = capture threshold (acute V usually <1 V).
  4. Set output ≥5 V or 3× threshold.
SENSING THRESHOLD (SENSITIVITY)
  1. Demand mode; rate below intrinsic (beats visible, box inhibited).
  2. Raise the number (↓ sensitivity) until sense LED stops / reverts to asynchronous — do not sustain (R-on-T).
  3. Lower the number until LED tracks each beat = sensing threshold.
  4. Set ≈ ½ threshold. No rhythm → 2 mV. (adequate: R>5, P>1.5–2 mV)
Reminder: LOWER number = MORE sensitive.

⚠ EMERGENCY / CRASH PACE
  1. Mode VVI (or DOO if interference) · Rate 80 · Output MAX (10 V) · Sensitivity nominal.
  2. Confirm MECHANICAL capture (art/SpO₂), not ECG alone.
  3. No capture → transcutaneous pace + defib; treat K⁺, acidosis, hypoxia.
DAILY CHECK PROTOCOL — in this order, every shift & after any change stop at any failed step and fix it first
1SAFETY — gloves, discharge static, insulated wire ends, defib + transcutaneous pads in reach. ECG monitor in pacing / spike-enhanced mode.
2GENERATOR — ON, unlocked, battery OK, connections seated, A/V ports correct. Record current mode / rate / outputs / sensitivities.
3CONFIRM CAPTURE NOW — each spike → P/QRS and a pulse on art / SpO₂. If not → Loss-of-Capture ladder (p4) before any threshold test.
4UNDERLYING RHYTHM (pause test) — briefly drop rate below intrinsic (or ≈40). Note rhythm & rate. No escape rhythm → STOP: do not test thresholds.
5CAPTURE THRESHOLD — VVI/AAI +10 bpm; ↓ output to loss, ↑ to return. Record A & V.
6SET OUTPUT — ≥5 V or 3× threshold (greater). Re-confirm mechanical capture.
7SENSING THRESHOLD — rate below intrinsic; ↑ number until LED stops, ↓ until it tracks every beat. Record A & V.
8SET SENSITIVITY — ≈ ½ threshold (no rhythm → 2 mV). Watch 30 s: no competing spikes, no pauses.
9RESTORE intended mode & rate · LOCK · set alarm limits.
10DOCUMENT & TREND — flowsheet (p6). Rising threshold vs yesterday → anticipate failure; day 4–5 → consider PPM.

TEMPORARY EPICARDIAL PACINGWAVEFORM RECOGNITION & ADVANCED TECHNIQUES · PAGE 2 / 6
PACED-ECG RECOGNITION red line = pacing artefact (spike)
ATRIAL PACED (AAI)
Spike → P wave → conducted narrow QRS. Atrial capture with intact AV conduction.
VENTRICULAR PACED (VVI)
Spike → wide QRS (LBBB-like) with discordant T. No preceding P.
AV SEQUENTIAL (DDD/DVI)
Two spikes: atrial → P, then ventricular → wide QRS. Restores AV synchrony / atrial kick.
FUSION
Spike coincides with intrinsic depolarisation → hybrid QRS. Normal, not a fault.
PSEUDOFUSION
Spike falls on intrinsic QRS, morphology unchanged. Normal; do not mistake for failure to capture.
FAILURE TO CAPTURE
Spike with no following P/QRS. Confirm true (spike-enhance), ↑ output, check reversibles/circuit.
UNDERSENSING
Spike fires despite intrinsic beat → competing pacing, R-on-T risk. ↑ sensitivity (LOWER number).
OVERSENSING
Inappropriate inhibition → unexpected pause (e.g. T-wave / diathermy sensed). ↓ sensitivity (RAISE number).
ADVANCED TECHNIQUES
ATRIAL ELECTROGRAM (AEG) — DIAGNOSE THE RHYTHM
  1. Use the atrial epicardial wires as recording electrodes to magnify atrial activity.
  2. Unipolar: one atrial wire → a chest (V) lead. Bipolar: two atrial wires → RA & LA limb leads, read lead I.
  3. Reveals P–QRS relationship → SVT vs VT, flutter, AV dissociation, diagnoses broad-complex tachycardia.
  4. Label the trace; keep skin/leads insulated (micro-shock).
ATRIAL OVERDRIVE / BURST PACING — FLUTTER
  1. Type I atrial flutter / re-entrant SVT with atrial wires that capture.
  2. AAI at 10–20% above the flutter rate, or burst ~400–600/min for a few seconds, then abruptly stop.
  3. Aim: capture the circuit & interrupt re-entry. Risk: induces AF — have defib ready; not for AF.
AV DELAY · CROSS-TALK · SAFETY PACING
  1. AV delay (DDD) ~120–200 ms: shorter → more V pacing; longer → favours intrinsic conduction. Optimise for filling/CO.
  2. Cross-talk: atrial output oversensed by the V channel → inappropriate V inhibition. Guarded by blanking/refractory periods.
  3. Ventricular safety pacing: committed V pace at short AV (~110 ms) if a signal is sensed early — prevents asystole from cross-talk.
PMT · RUNAWAY · WEANING
  1. PMT (endless-loop, tracking modes via retrograde VA): terminate with magnet / DOO, switch to DDI/VVI, or ↑ PVARP.
  2. Runaway pacemaker (rare generator fault, rapid pacing): disconnect wires / replace box — emergency.
  3. Weaning: drop rate below intrinsic; confirm stable escape rhythm + haemodynamics; leave back-up at 40.

READING A PACED 12-LEAD
  1. RV apex (usual temporary site) → LBBB morphology + left/superior axis: II, III, aVF negative (QS); I, aVL positive.
  2. Precordial: LBBB pattern — V1–V3 dominantly negative.
  3. RVOT pacing → LBBB + inferior axis (II/III/aVF positive, tall R).
  4. LV / epicardial wires → RBBB-like (dominant R in V1).
  5. Expect appropriate discordance (ST/T opposite to main QRS). Narrow or absent discordance → suspect fusion / pseudofusion / lead displacement.
  6. Diagnosing ischaemia in a paced/LBBB rhythm → Sgarbossa criteria (concordant ST↑, or excessive discordant ST↑).
MODE SWITCH — DDD DURING ATRIAL TACHYARRHYTHMIA appropriate vs failure modes
✓ APPROPRIATE — switch DDD→DDI, ventricular rate capped
✗ TRACKING — fails to switch → rapid ventricular pacing
✗ FALSE SWITCH — atrial oversensing (far-field R / myopotentials)
ON THE TEMPORARY BOX
  1. Fast atrial rhythm tracked → manually switch DDD → DDI or VVI; consider ↓ upper-rate limit.
  2. Then treat the arrhythmia: atrial overdrive/burst (flutter), rate control, or DC cardioversion.
  3. False switch (loss of AV synchrony in true sinus) → reduce atrial sensitivity (raise number), check lead contact.

TEMPORARY EPICARDIAL PACINGLOSS OF CAPTURE — STRUCTURED FIX · PAGE 4 / 6
LOSS OF CAPTURE — spike present but no myocardial response (electrical & / or mechanical) work top → bottom, in parallel if compromised
⚠ TRAP — AT MAX SENSITIVITY & LOSING CAPTURE · SENSITIVITY IS NOT THE CAPTURE DIAL
› Sensitivity = input (sensing); capture = output. Turning sensitivity ↑ cannot restore capture.
› At MAX sensitivity you oversense (T-wave / myopotential / diathermy) → box inhibits output → missing spikes, easily misread as 'lost capture'.
› Spike, no P/QRS → true capture failure → work the OUTPUT ladder; sensitivity is irrelevant.
› Spikes missing / pauses → oversensing → reduce sensitivity (RAISE the number), not raise it.
› If V-dependent → go VOO at MAX output. Asynchronous = no sensing → removes over- and under-sensing at a stroke.
› Captures in VOO → it was a sensing problem, not capture; once stable reset sensitivity ≈ ½ threshold.
› No capture in VOO at max output → true capture failure → emergency: transcutaneous → transvenous → iso / adrenaline; treat reversibles; call for help.
› Already at max sensitivity ⇒ functional non-capture (undersensing) is excluded → it's output / circuit / excitability.

REVERSIBLE CONTRIBUTORS (raise threshold / exit block)
  1. Electrolytes: hyperkalaemia (key), hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia.
  2. Acid–base / gas: acidosis, hypoxia, hypercapnia.
  3. Drugs: class I (flecainide) & III antiarrhythmics; others.
  4. Metabolic: hypothermia, hypothyroidism.
  5. Myocardial: ischaemia / infarction; interface oedema / fibrosis (day 4+); perforation.
MIMICS — is it really loss of capture?
  1. No spike at all → failure to OUTPUT (battery / lead / oversensing), not capture.
  2. Spike in refractory tissue from undersensing → functional non-capture → fix sensing.
  3. Spike on intrinsic QRS, unchanged complex → pseudofusion (normal).
  4. Progressive ↑ output over days → exit block / fibrosis → plan PPM.
⚠ NO ESCAPE RHYTHM = PERI-ARREST
  1. Persistent non-capture + no underlying rhythm → treat as arrest pathway.
  2. Transcutaneous pace now → transvenous temporary wire; iso / adrenaline bridge.
  3. Treat reversibles aggressively; urgent surgical / cardiology review; ?permanent system.
CAPTURE — REMEMBER
Chamber-specific
Atrial capture is harder; atrial wires fail earlier (>60% R, >80% L by day 5).		 Electrical ≠ mechanical
Confirm capture on art / SpO₂, never the ECG spike alone.		 Thresholds drift up
Interface oedema / fibrosis from ~day 4 → output ≥5 V or 3× threshold.		 Undersensing masquerade

TEMPORARY EPICARDIAL PACINGSENSING FAULTS — STRUCTURED FIX · PAGE 5 / 6
SENSING FAULTS — the box sees too much (over) or too little (under). Direction rule: LOWER number = MORE sensitive. fix the side that matches the trace
OVERSENSING — inappropriate inhibition → pauses fix = RAISE the number
1RECOGNISE — unexpected pauses / missing spikes, rate below set rate, sense LED flashing without a P/QRS. Dependent patient → may be asystole.
2PROTECT — if dependent or symptomatic: switch to VOO / DOO (asynchronous) now. Pads on, defib ready.
3IDENTIFY THE SIGNAL — T-wave? far-field R (atrial channel)? myopotentials (shiver, movement)? diathermy / electrical interference? loose connection noise?
4↓ SENSITIVITY — RAISE the number stepwise (e.g. 2 → 3 → 5 mV) until pauses stop and intrinsic beats are still sensed.
5REMOVE THE SOURCE — stop shivering/movement, bipolar diathermy & short bursts, re-route cables, reseat connections, change ECG cable.
6STILL OVERSENSING → try the other wire pair / bipolar config; leave asynchronous if dependent; senior review.
7RE-MEASURE & DOCUMENT — repeat sensing threshold; reset ≈ ½; log change & cause.
UNDERSENSING — missed beats → competing spikes fix = LOWER the number
1RECOGNISE — spikes marching through intrinsic beats, competing paced & native QRS, spikes landing on T (R-on-T), sense LED not flashing with native beats.
2PROTECT — R-on-T risk: defib ready. If native rhythm adequate, temporarily ↓ rate below intrinsic so spikes stop competing while you fix.
3↑ SENSITIVITY — LOWER the number stepwise (e.g. 5 → 3 → 2 → 1 mV) until LED tracks every native beat.
4SIGNAL TOO SMALL? — re-measure threshold; adequate R >5 mV, P >1.5–2 mV. Small signal → poor contact / fibrosis / infarct / electrolytes.
5CHANGE THE CIRCUIT — reseat connections; switch wire pair; bipolar ↔ unipolar (skin electrode as indifferent).
6CAN'T SENSE RELIABLY → if native rhythm sufficient, pace below it (back-up ~40); if dependent, accept VOO/DOO & plan transvenous / PPM.
7RE-MEASURE & DOCUMENT — threshold, setting, cause. Check for functional non-capture that the undersensing was causing.
WHEN TO GO ASYNCHRONOUS (VOO / DOO)
  1. Dependent patient with oversensing pauses — immediately.
  2. Diathermy / electrocautery in use.
  3. Unable to find a sensitivity that both tracks native beats and avoids pauses.
  4. Never leave asynchronous with a competing native rhythm unless unavoidable — R-on-T risk; defib ready.
ATRIAL-CHANNEL SPECIFICS (DDD)
  1. Atrial oversensing of far-field R / myopotentials → false mode-switch or inappropriate inhibition → raise atrial number; check PVAB/blanking.
  2. Atrial undersensing → box paces atrium into native P → loss of AV synchrony; tracking failures → lower atrial number.
  3. Rapid atrial rhythm tracked at upper rate → switch DDI/VVI, then treat the arrhythmia.
CROSS-TALK & SAFETY PACING
  1. Cross-talk: atrial stimulus sensed on the V channel → V output inhibited → ventricular asystole in CHB.
  2. Protective: ventricular blanking after A-pace + safety pacing (committed V pace at ~110 ms) on some boxes.
  3. If suspected: ↓ atrial output, ↑ V number slightly, or DOO/DVI; confirm V capture mechanically.
SENSING — REMEMBER
Number direction
Lower mV = more sensitive. Oversense → raise. Undersense → lower.		 Set ≈ ½ threshold
Margin for small signals & fibrosis. Adequate R >5, P >1.5–2 mV.		 Max sensitivity is a trap
Lowest number → T-wave / noise oversensing → pauses that mimic lost capture.		 Asynchronous = no sensing
VOO/DOO cuts both faults at a stroke; use for dependence & diathermy, defib ready.
